# Round 6: Behavioral, Hiring Manager and HR

Usually 30 to 60 minutes. The interviewer wants to know how you work with people, handle problems and grow. Strong candidates give specific stories, not general statements.

## The STAR method (plus a L)

- **S**ituation: one or two sentences of context.
- **T**ask: what you were responsible for.
- **A**ction: what YOU did, step by step (use "I", not only "we"). This is the longest part.
- **R**esult: the outcome, with a number if you can.
- **L**earning: what you would do differently. Short.

Aim for 2 minutes per story.

## Build a story bank

Prepare 8 to 10 true stories and reuse them across questions: a production incident, a performance win, a disagreement, a mistake you owned, a tight deadline, mentoring someone, a migration, an unclear task, feedback you received. Use the worksheet to map stories to questions.

## If you lack experience

Use projects, open source, study projects, internships or team work outside tech. Be honest about the scope. Show how you learn: "I had not used streams, so I read the docs, built a small copy tool, and measured memory".

## Delivery tips

- Keep answers to about 2 minutes. Pause, then answer.
- Say "I" for your actions.
- On video calls, look at the camera when you finish a point, and keep notes out of sight.
- Never blame a person. Talk about the situation and what you changed.

## Red flags to avoid

Blaming others, no examples, badmouthing a previous employer, claiming you never fail, vague answers with no result.

## One-week routine

Day 1 to 2: write your stories. Day 3 to 4: practise aloud and record. Day 5: practise the "tell me about yourself" and "why this company" answers. Day 6: questions to ask them. Day 7: rest and review.
